  1. 1關於國民教育之敘述，下列何者正確？

    1. (A) 是一種權利
    2. (B) 目的在於培養服從權威領導的順民
    3. (C) 公民教育應完全由國家主導
    4. (D) 教育只是使國民習得謀生技能，與其人格發展無關

    正解：(A)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (A) 憲法第 21 條規定人民有受國民教育之權利與義務，兼具權利與義務雙重性質；人民得據以請求國家提供基本教育機會，故稱其為一種權利正確。

    ❌ (B) 國民教育目的在培養健全人格、民主素養與獨立思考的現代公民，不是製造服從權威的順民。

    ❌ (C) 憲法第 162 條國家對教育機關僅為依法監督，另有大學自治、私人興學與家長教育選擇權，不可能完全由國家主導。

    ❌ (D) 教育基本法第 2 條明定教育在培養人民健全人格、民主素養與人文涵養，與人格發展密切相關，不只是謀生技能。

    📚 出處：憲法第 21、158、162 條；教育基本法第 2 條。

  2. 2下列有關憲法工作權之敘述，何者正確？

    1. (A) 公務員甲在假日期間至行天宮兼職擔任算命師貼補家用，不屬於工作權保障範圍
    2. (B) 法律規定計程車駕駛必須經考試取得執業證照，屬於對工作權之客觀許可條件之限制
    3. (C) 法律規定律師出庭應穿律師袍，係屬於對職業執行方式之限制
    4. (D) 人民失業有請求國家給予適當工作之權利

    正解：(C)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (C) 穿律師袍不影響誰能當律師，只規範「這個職業要怎麼執行」，屬職業自由三階理論中強度最低的職業執行方式限制，有正當公益理由即可。

    ❌ (A) 工作權保障人民所選擇的任何正當職業，假日兼職算命本身即在保障範圍內；公務員兼職受限是基於公務員身分的另一層限制，不是不受保障。

    ❌ (B) 須考試取得執照屬「主觀許可條件」——當事人可經努力達成；客觀條件指與個人能力無關者，如名額上限、距離限制。

    ❌ (D) 憲法第 15 條工作權不含請求國家給予具體工作的權利，國家僅負創造就業環境、提供職訓等保護義務。

    📚 出處：憲法第 15 條；職業自由三階理論（司法院釋字第 584、649、711 號）。

  3. 3依憲法、增修條文及司法院大法官解釋，關於總統機密特權，下列敘述何者錯誤？

    1. (A) 總統因行使憲法職權而獲得之資訊，如公開可能影響國家安全與國家利益者，總統有決定不予公開之權力
    2. (B) 總統如以書面釋明，在訴訟程序中公開機密資訊有妨害國家利益之虞，檢察官及法院應予以尊重，不得駁回總統之決定
    3. (C) 在刑事訴訟程序上，總統就國家機密事項有拒絕證言權
    4. (D) 總統在訴訟程序中公開機密資訊是否有妨害國家利益之虞，應僅由承辦檢察官或審判庭法官依保密程序為之50720-5102051220-51320

    正解：(B)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (B) 錯在「不得駁回」。釋字第 627 號指出總統雖得以書面釋明，但檢察官或法院仍應審酌釋明是否充分，認理由不足時得為駁回之裁定，並非一律受總統決定拘束。

    ❌ (A) 該號解釋承認總統就行使憲法職權所獲資訊，於公開將影響國家安全與利益時，有決定不予公開的機密特權。

    ❌ (C) 總統就國家機密事項在刑事訴訟程序中得拒絕證言、拒絕提交相關物件，是機密特權的具體展現。

    ❌ (D) 解釋明定此類審酌應僅由承辦檢察官或審判庭法官依保密程序為之，不得公開，敘述正確。

    📚 出處：司法院釋字第 627 號（總統國家機密特權）。

  56. With so many people crossing borders every day, how do we know we’re not letting dangerous people into our countries? One response is biometric identification technology. It has been developed to recognize individuals and to identify criminals. In fact, biometric identification is not a new invention. Law enforcement agencies have been using photographs and fingerprints as biometric identifiers since the late 19th century. Today, biometric identification technology, including both physiological and behavioral biometrics, has become increasingly prevalent as a means of enhancing security and protecting individuals' interests. 50720-51020 51220-51320 Physiological biometrics, such as fingerprints and facial features, use human morphology to identify or recognize individuals. Besides fingerprint scanners, there are software programs that identify faces, palms, and irises. Scanning these physical features ensures that the person being scanned is who he or she claims to be. Unlike personal identification numbers, which are used to access bank accounts, biometric identifiers are extremely difficult, if not impossible, for criminals to steal. Behavioral biometrics can also be used to identify people. Certain behaviors are unique to individuals, such as their speech or the way they type. The classic behavioral biometric marker is a person's signature, but it can be problematic as it can be copied. However, people do automatically recognize subtleties in the way a person speaks, such as intonation and regional accents. Typing patterns, likewise, would be very difficult to observe and mimic convincingly. Biometrics can have two potential applications: identification and identity verification. Identification uses biometric information to discover the identity of an unknown person. DNA evidence has joined fingerprints as a common tool of law enforcement for this purpose. Today, we use passports to verify our identity when crossing borders, but passports can be stolen or forged. An effective biometric identification system would be difficult to fool. Because of this, many countries are considering biometric additions to or replacements of existing identification system. India, for instance, has already implemented such a system, which utilizes fingerprint and iris scans, in addition to photographs. While biometric identification technology shows promise in various applications, there are ethical concerns surrounding the development of biometric identification technology. Critics worry about the possibility of criminal uses of the technology, as legitimate organizations can use biometric scanners to verify personal information. And civil liberties advocates are concerned about the potential for abuse by authorities. Therefore, policymakers will need to balance security and law enforcement against personal freedom and privacy. Ethical considerations must be taken into account when developing biometric identification technology.
