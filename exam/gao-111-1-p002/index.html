  1. 1下列何者為憲法第 1 條明文規定之國體？

    1. (A) 共和國
    2. (B) 自由國
    3. (C) 聯邦國
    4. (D) 法治國

    正解：(A)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (A) 憲法第 1 條規定「中華民國基於三民主義，為民有民治民享之民主共和國」，明文的國體是民主共和國。

    ❌ (B) 自由國不是憲法用語。

    ❌ (C) 我國是單一國，非聯邦國。

    ❌ (D) 法治國是憲法原則而非第 1 條所定的國體。

    📚 出處：憲法第 1 條

  2. 2平均地權條例規定，土地所有權移轉時，須依法申報現值，其憲法上之理由為何？

    1. (A) 因勞力資本投入而增值土地，應課徵土地增值稅
    2. (B) 履行人民之協力義務，以符漲價歸公目的
    3. (C) 中華民國領土內之土地屬於國民全體
    4. (D) 附著土地之礦隨同移轉

    正解：(B)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (B) 申報土地現值是為使漲價部分能課徵土地增值稅、落實憲法第 143 條「漲價歸公」，屬人民的協力義務。

    ❌ (A) 因勞力資本投入而增值的部分依憲法規定應歸私人所有，不課增值稅。

    ❌ (C) 土地屬於國民全體是所有權的基本原則，非申報現值的直接理由。

    ❌ (D) 礦產隨土地移轉與現值申報無關。

    📚 出處：憲法第 143 條；平均地權條例

  3. 3依憲法第 8 條及相關司法院大法官解釋意旨，關於人身自由，下列敘述何者錯誤？

    1. (A) 所謂「法官保留原則」，係指對於人民之審問與處罰，僅限於具有審判權之法官所構成之獨任或合議之法院，始得為之
    2. (B) 傳染病防治之強制隔離措施，其所須踐行之正當法律程序，亦須與刑事處罰之限制被告人身自由一樣交由法院決定，不得由衛生主管機關逕行決定
    3. (C) 法務部行政執行署所屬人員依行政執行法規定，對於欠稅不繳之納稅義務人進行拘提與管收，並不違反憲法第 8 條第 1 項規定
    4. (D) 對於經常逃學或逃家之少年所進行之強制收容，仍須經法院加以裁定後，始得為之

    正解：(B)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (B) 錯誤在此。釋字第 690 號認為傳染病強制隔離具緊急與專業性質，由主管機關逕行處置、事後有救濟途徑即符合正當程序，不須事前法院決定。

    ❌ (A) 正確。釋字第 392 號界定法院為有審判權的法官所構成者。

    ❌ (C) 正確。釋字第 588 號認拘提管收的程序（經法院裁定）合憲。

    ❌ (D) 正確。逃學逃家少年的收容須經法院裁定（釋字第 664 號）。

    📚 出處：司法院釋字第 392、588、664、690 號解釋

  46. Hikikomori is a psychological condition which makes people shut themselves off from society, often staying in their houses for months on end. There are at least half a million of them in Japan. It was once thought of as a young person’s condition, but sufferers are getting older and staying locked away for longer. It is an economic as well as a social threat to the country, and is seriously worrying Japanese government. As of 2015, there were 541,000 hikikomori aged 15-39 in Japan, according to government statistics. There is no data on other age groups, suggesting that the figure is likely to be far larger. Some families are also loath to report hikikomori in their households, Kingston said. Doctors began to observe hikikomori as a social phenomenon around the mid-1980s, when young men exhibited signs of lethargy, refused to communicate, and spent most of their time in their rooms. There’s no unifying reason why people become hikikomori. Some withdraw from society because they feel they don’t know what to do with their lives and can no longer cope with the pressure from people around them. Others are triggered by events in their lives, like bad grades or heartbreak. Hikikomori feel a deep sense of shame that they cannot work at a job like ordinary people. They think of themselves as worthless and unqualified for happiness. Almost all feel remorse at having betrayed their parents’ expectations. As hikikomori refuse to participate in society, let alone go to work, Japan’s economy also suffers. They diminish the size of the workforce, so they contribute to a tighter labor market. Also, they are not self-sufficient, so when family support dries up due to death or financial problems, they will need to rely on state assistance. Japanese government plans to set up counseling centers and have support staff visit hikikomori at home in a bid to boost the country’s flagging workforce. But some criticize this may put pressure on hikikomori. Professor Jeff Kingston, an Asian studies professor in Tokyo, said, “One can only hope that more access to various therapies and public health campaigns to destigmatize the phenomenon will encourage more to seek help, find it, and learn to manage their symptoms so that they can lead more productive and fulfilling lives.”
