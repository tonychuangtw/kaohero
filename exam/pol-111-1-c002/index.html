  1. 1During the summer there is always a shortage of cheap ＿＿＿ in Kenting area, so you’d better make a reservationfor the room you want in advance.

    1. (A) affluence
    2. (B) accommodation
    3. (C) attraction
    4. (D) adaptation

    正解：(B)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (B) accommodation 指「住宿、住處」，與後半句 make a reservation for the room 呼應；句意是墾丁夏天便宜的住宿總是不足，最好事先訂房。

    ❌ (A) affluence 是「富裕、豐足」，指財富充裕的狀態，不能拿來訂房。

    ❌ (C) attraction 是「觀光景點、吸引力」，景點不會不夠而要先預訂房間。

    ❌ (D) adaptation 是「適應」或作品「改編」，與住宿無關。

    📚 出處：英文字彙；accommodation 與 make a reservation 的旅遊情境搭配。

  2. 2As science and ＿＿＿ advance, many people enjoy a higher and higher standard of living.

    1. (A) foundation
    2. (B) texture
    3. (C) technology
    4. (D) fantasy

    正解：(C)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (C) technology 是「科技」，science and technology 為固定並列搭配；句意是隨著科學與科技進步，許多人享有愈來愈高的生活水準。

    ❌ (A) foundation 是「基礎、基金會」，不與 science 並列表示進步。

    ❌ (B) texture 是「質地、觸感」，指材料表面感受，語意不合。

    ❌ (D) fantasy 是「幻想」，無法與 advance（進步）連用形容社會發展。

    📚 出處：英文字彙；science and technology 固定搭配。

  3. 3Even though the rent for the apartment was quite low, I found that the cost of its ＿＿＿ was high.

    1. (A) integrities
    2. (B) privileges
    3. (C) statements
    4. (D) utilities

    正解：(D)

    逐題詳解（免費試讀）

    ✅ (D) utilities 複數時指「水電瓦斯等公用事業費用」；句意是房租雖低，但水電等費用很高，正好與句首 Even though 的轉折呼應。

    ❌ (A) integrities 是「正直、完整性」，屬抽象品格概念，且通常不可數。

    ❌ (B) privileges 是「特權」，不是居住要付的開銷。

    ❌ (C) statements 是「陳述、對帳單」，the cost of its statements 不成語意。

    📚 出處：英文字彙；rent（房租）與 utilities（水電費）的居住費用區分。

  16. We all think we understand the formula for keeping healthy-- eat right, exercise, don’t smoke, skip dessert. But that picture is vastly incomplete, perhaps fatally so. While our individual health is highly dependent on our daily behaviors, it may be even more strongly influenced by the social and environmental conditions in which we live. Smith, a prestigious doctor, argues that the future of our health, and our healthcare system, depends on growing and supporting a new generation of healthcare practitioners who look upriver at the source of our health problems, rather than simply opting for quick-hit symptomatic relief. These “Upstreamists,” as he calls them, are practitioners on the frontlines of health who see that health (like sickness) is more than a chemical equation that can be balanced with pills and procedures administered within clinic walls. They see, rather, that health begins in our everyday lives, in the places where we live, work, eat and play. Upstreamists — who may be doctors, nurses or other clinicians — know that asthma can start in the air around us, or from the mold in the walls of our homes. They understand that obesity, diabetes and heart disease partly originate in our busy modern schedules, in the unhealthy food choices available in our stores and even in the way our neighborhoods are designed. They believe that depression, anxiety and high blood pressure can arise from chronically stressful conditions at work and home. And, just as important, these caregivers understand how to translate this knowledge into meaningful action.
